Since 8 July 2026, a business for which work is performed may apply to the Chief Labour Inspector for an individual interpretation. The interpretation determines whether the legal relationship described in the application constitutes an employment relationship within the meaning of Article 22 § 1 of the Labour Code. The legal basis is Article 14b of the Act on the National Labour Inspectorate, added by the Act of 11 March 2026 (Journal of Laws 2026, item 473). The interpretation is presented as a protective umbrella in the event of an inspection. The question is where the limits of that protection lie.

## What does an interpretation protect against?

Above all, against sanctions. Under Article 14b(16) of the Act on the National Labour Inspectorate, the applicant "may not be subject to administrative or financial sanctions or penalties to the extent that it has complied with the individual interpretation obtained". The interpretation itself is not binding on the applicant, but by departing from it, the applicant loses that protection.

The interpretation is, however, binding on the authorities of the National Labour Inspectorate and may be changed or revoked only in the event of a change in the circumstances of the case (Article 14b(17)). An application may concern both ongoing cooperation and a model that has yet to be implemented.

## When will an interpretation not help?

To put it plainly: when reality departs from the facts presented in the application. Article 14b(5) allows the labour inspection authorities to assess, in the course of an inspection, the actual nature of the legal relationship if the facts established during the inspection differ from those described in the application. If the application describes independent cooperation, but in practice the contractor works under the direction of a superior, at a place and time set by that superior, the interpretation will not work.

An interpretation is not issued in respect of those elements of the facts which, on the date the application is filed, are the subject of pending administrative proceedings conducted by the labour inspection authorities or the Social Insurance Institution (Article 14b(1)).

Since a change in the circumstances of the case allows the interpretation to be changed or revoked, the protection lasts only as long as the cooperation looks the way it was described in the application.

## Does an interpretation bind ZUS, the tax authorities and the courts?

The Act provides that the interpretation binds the authorities of the National Labour Inspectorate. Of other authorities it says only that the Social Insurance Institution and the National Revenue Administration receive it (Article 14b(18)). The Act does not provide for them to be bound by the Chief Labour Inspector's assessment.

Nor does it provide for a court to be bound. If the contractor brings an action for a declaration that an employment relationship exists, the court will assess the cooperation independently. Under Article 22 § 1¹ of the Labour Code, employment under the conditions set out in § 1 is employment on the basis of an employment relationship, regardless of the name of the contract concluded by the parties.

The protection under Article 14b(16) concerns sanctions. If the contract is found to be an employment contract, it does not cancel the amounts due as such, such as social security contributions or tax.

## Practical conclusions

An interpretation makes sense, but subject to three conditions. The application must describe the cooperation as it really is, not merely as the contract describes it. It must be filed before proceedings are opened in the matter before the labour inspectorate or ZUS. And once it has been obtained, practice must remain consistent with the description, because any change in the way the cooperation is carried out weakens the protection.

An interpretation kept in a binder will not replace what the cooperation looks like day to day. It binds the labour inspectorate and protects against sanctions, but only as long as reality matches the description presented.
