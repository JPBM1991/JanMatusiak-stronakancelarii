The holiday season brings record passenger volumes and, with them, delays, cancellations and overbooked aircraft. What is worth knowing is that a disruption of this kind triggers a right to **concrete financial compensation - up to EUR 600 per person** - regardless of the price of the ticket. These rights apply not only to leisure travellers: business professionals who fly regularly can also pursue them. The topic is particularly timely right now, as the European Union is finalising a long-awaited reform of air passenger rights.

## When Are You Entitled to Compensation and How Much?

The basis is Regulation (EC) No 261/2004, which applies directly across the European Union. Compensation is due in three situations:

- your flight arrived at the destination with a delay of **at least three hours**,
- your flight was cancelled and the airline notified you fewer than fourteen days before departure (excluding cases of extraordinary circumstances),
- you were denied boarding - for example, due to overbooking.

The amount depends on the distance of the route: **EUR 250** for flights up to 1,500 km, **EUR 400** for routes between 1,500 and 3,500 km, and **EUR 600** for longer flights. Importantly, the amount does not depend on what you paid for the ticket - a promotion fare worth a few dozen zlotys entitles you to exactly the same sum. The rules cover scheduled flights, low-cost carriers and charter flights booked through a travel agency.

## When Can the Airline Refuse to Pay?

A carrier can escape liability only if the disruption was caused by **extraordinary circumstances** - and the burden of proving this rests with the airline. Circumstances considered extraordinary are external events outside the airline's control: extreme weather, airspace closure, bird strike or a strike by third-party service providers. If the cause was a technical fault detected during routine maintenance or an understaffed flight, the airline generally cannot invoke force majeure.

## Why Is This Particularly Worth Remembering Right Now?

After more than a decade of negotiations, the EU is closing the reform of passenger rights. For travellers, the most important thing is what the reform did **not** change: despite earlier proposals to raise the delay threshold to four or even six hours, the right to compensation after three hours and the existing amounts from EUR 250 to EUR 600 were retained.

In Poland, a claim for this compensation **expires after one year** from the date of the flight - a shorter period than in many other countries. If a disrupted flight from last year's holiday is still unresolved, the time to pursue it is running out.

## How to Claim Compensation?

The process follows clear steps:

1. **Submit a claim directly to the airline** - describe the flight and state the amount you are seeking. The airline is required to respond.
2. If it refuses or does not reply - submit a complaint to the **passenger rights ombudsman** at the President of the Civil Aviation Authority.
3. As a last resort - **court proceedings**, in which the court can order payment together with costs.

It is worth keeping all documentation: ticket purchase confirmation, correspondence with the airline, and any receipts for expenses incurred as a result of the delay.
