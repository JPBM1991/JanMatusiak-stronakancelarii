From 3 December 2026, documentary form will be sufficient to grant a power of attorney to participate in a shareholders' meeting of a limited liability company. As a rule, an e-mail will do. The change is being presented as another step towards reducing formalities.

## What exactly changes?

The basis is the Act of 4 September 2026 amending the Commercial Companies Code and the Act amending the Commercial Companies Code and certain other acts (Journal of Laws 2026, item 1440), promulgated on 2 November 2026. It enters into force thirty days after promulgation, i.e. on 3 December 2026.

Article 243 § 2 of the Commercial Companies Code currently reads: "The power of attorney should be granted in writing on pain of nullity. A copy of the power of attorney is attached to the book of minutes." Only the first sentence is being amended, and will read: "The power of attorney should be granted in documentary form, unless the articles of association provide for stricter requirements for granting it."

The second change concerns Article 238 § 1 of the Commercial Companies Code. A shareholder's consent to being notified of a meeting by electronic mail or to an electronic delivery address will no longer require written form, but documentary form. A sentence has likewise been added under which the articles of association may provide for stricter conditions for granting consent.

The third change covers Article 300⁹⁵ § 2 of the Commercial Companies Code and extends the same rule to the simple joint-stock company.

The articles of association may provide for stricter requirements. Whether it is worth introducing them depends above all on the ownership structure of the particular company.

## Does this apply to every company?

The change in question covers only the limited liability company and the simple joint-stock company.

In practice, from December there will be a statutory two-track system. A proxy at a shareholders' meeting of a limited liability company will be able to rely on a power of attorney granted in documentary form, while a proxy at a general meeting of a joint-stock company will still need a document in written form, and that on pain of nullity (Article 412¹ § 1 of the Commercial Companies Code). In a capital group running companies in different forms, this may mean, for example, two separate standards for handling documents.

## Why does removing the sanction of nullity matter?

The previous wording of Article 243 § 2 of the Commercial Companies Code stipulated written form on pain of nullity. A power of attorney granted in any other way was invalid, and the proxy could not vote effectively. The new wording requires documentary form, but no longer stipulates the sanction of nullity.

Under Article 73 § 1 of the Civil Code, an act performed without the form stipulated by statute is invalid only where the statute provides for the sanction of nullity. The effect is that the centre of gravity of a dispute shifts from whether the power of attorney is valid to whether the authority can be demonstrated. That is a significant change, not merely a cosmetic one. For the practice of shareholders' meetings it means more than the mere admissibility of granting a power of attorney in the body of an e-mail.

## Do existing documents need to be updated?

There is no such obligation. The amending act contains a transitional provision. Consents to electronic notification given before the date of entry into force, and powers of attorney granted before that date, remain subject to the previous provisions. Nothing expires and nothing needs to be renewed.
